# Review: from-proto-agent-to-person

## Verdict
Accept with revisions. This is a competent, scrupulously sourced synthesis whose citations are real, correctly attributed, and unusually well fact-checked (the provenance trail even documents three corrected errors). It is not rejectable on rigor or honesty. But it should not be accepted as-is, because its central weakness is structural: the contribution is integrative rather than novel, the "predictions" of §14 are largely retrodictions of the very studies it assembles, and the nearest genuine rival framework — neuroconstructivism / developmental systems theory — is never named, so the paper's signature "neither nativism nor environmentalism" move knocks down two strawmen no serious developmentalist holds. The single most serious problem is that the architecture is hard to falsify and is not differentiated from the existing interactionist consensus it restates.

## Claim grounding

- **"each additional year of schooling raises measured intelligence by roughly one to five IQ points" (§11)** → supported. Matches Ritchie & Tucker-Drob exactly.

- **"The difference is principally the quantity and quality of external cognitive structure that the agent has had the opportunity to internalize, far more than brain size or general intelligence." (§11)** → **overstated.** A 1–5 IQ-point/year schooling effect does not license "principally … far more than … general intelligence." This is a strong causal-decomposition claim about the deprived-survivor-vs-scientist spread, and the cited evidence is modest and about schooling's marginal effect, not about partitioning the total variance against heritable general intelligence (which the behavior-genetic literature puts substantial weight on, and which the paper does not engage).

- **"the offset of the amnesia tracks the development of language and narrative skill rather than the development of memory storage as such" (§9)** → **unsupported and one-sided.** Asserted with no citation, and it dismisses a well-developed competing account. There is a substantial neurogenic hypothesis (high infant hippocampal neurogenesis degrading early memory traces; Josselyn & Frankland and the *Learning & Memory* review literature) plus immature-consolidation accounts. The mainstream view is that these factors act *together*. The paper presents the language/narrative cause as established fact; it should be hedged and the neurobiological account acknowledged.

- **Abstract: "tests the integrated account against the boundary cases of institutional deprivation and late first-language acquisition."** → **overstated relative to the paper's own §12**, which concedes the cases "never isolate a single missing input" and are "ethically and methodologically compromised." Because rival interactionist accounts predict the same sparing/loss pattern, the boundary cases *corroborate* but cannot *test* (i.e., discriminate) the architecture. "Tests" should be "is consistent with" or "corroborated by."

- **"contingent social interaction rather than acoustic exposure alone drives early language learning (Kuhl, Tsao & Liu, 2003)" (§3/§6)** → supported. Accurate description of the live-vs-recorded Mandarin result.

- **Late-L1 claim, Mayberry & Lock (2003) (§7, §12)** → supported and correctly characterized ("accessibility rather than modality"). This is the paper's strongest single empirical anchor and it is used fairly.

- **Neonatal-imitation hedge (§3)** → well-grounded. The Oostenbroek null, the Meltzoff re-analysis dispute, and the Davis et al. heterogeneity are all correctly represented; the conclusion ("safer to speak of early sensorimotor resonance") is appropriately cautious.

- **"the development of the person … recapitulates that stratification under compression" (§11, also abstract, §5, §15)** → **unsupported analogy.** See Argument.

## Argument

- **§1, §15 (the framing dichotomy).** The paper positions itself as "neither nativism nor environmentalism." But "strong nativism" and "strong environmentalism" are strawmen — essentially nobody in contemporary developmental science holds either. The live competitors are *other* interactionist frameworks: probabilistic epigenesis / developmental systems theory (Gottlieb, Oyama) and especially **neuroconstructivism** (Karmiloff-Smith; Mareschal et al.), which already makes the experience-expectant, progressively-structuring, "domain-relevant-becomes-domain-specific" claims the paper rediscovers. Gap: none of these is cited or distinguished, so the reader cannot see what the bootstrapping architecture adds over the consensus it restates.

- **§14 (predictions).** A synthesis "is worth having only if it predicts more than its components do separately" — the paper's own test. Yet most listed predictions are restatements of the cited findings: "early joint-attention quality should predict later … narrative ability, and theory-of-mind" is roughly Carpenter et al. (1998); "elaborative parental reminiscing should predict autobiographical coherence" is Reese et al. (1993). These are retrodictions of the assembled evidence, not novel risk. Gap: the genuinely *new* predictions (e.g., that the private-speech→inner-speech transition *mediates* EF gains) are not clearly distinguished from the recycled ones, so the predictive payload is overstated.

- **Falsifiability (whole paper, acute in §13–§14).** The architecture is described as content-neutral, regressing under stress, developing unevenly, varying across cultures, and running "one algorithm on two niches." With that much flexibility it is unclear what observation would count against it. Importing the free-energy principle (§3) as the "unifying frame" inherits FEP's own well-known unfalsifiability complaints. Gap: the paper never states a clean disconfirming observation.

- **§5, §11, §15 (the Mauss recapitulation).** The claim that ontogeny "recapitulates, under compression," Mauss's historical sequence of the person concept is the softest load-bearing move and it recurs in the abstract and conclusion. The mapping (relational → narratable → juridical → rational) is *imposed* on the developmental sequence rather than demonstrated, the two sequences are categorically different (cultural-historical category vs individual cognitive capacity), and the move carries an unmistakable whiff of discredited recapitulationism. It is rhetorically attractive and evidentially empty; the paper would lose nothing scientific by demoting it to a one-line suggestive aside.

- **§4 (active-inference attachment).** "On this account the infant forms…" leans a generalization on Cittern et al. (2018), a single simulation. Fairly cited, but presented as if it "connects attachment to the same machinery" in an established way. It is one speculative model; the prose should mark it as such.

- **§11 (upper layers).** Evidence is heavily back-loaded onto infancy and early childhood. The "reflective self" and "institution-compatible adult" layers — central to the title's "to Person" — rest on assertion plus the one IQ meta-analysis. The mechanism by which schooling/law/science get "internalized as a capacity and a self-description" is named, not shown.

**What is genuinely strong (briefly):** The three-way moral/legal/developmental separation in §2 is disciplined and pre-empts the paper's most likely misreading. The hedging on contested findings is exemplary (neonatal imitation, the r = .22 correction, reminiscing as correlational). The deprivation double-dissociation argument in §12 — proto-agent and local adaptations spared, upper symbolic stack lost — is a real and well-constructed inference, and the late-L1 anchor (Mayberry & Lock) is correctly load-bearing.

## Factual errors
None found. I checked the suspicious quantitative claims (De Wolff & van IJzendoorn numbers, Ritchie & Tucker-Drob figures, Saffran's 8-month/2-minute detail, Wellman's age-4 consolidation, Rakoczy's ages 2–3, Mehler's days-old newborns) and they are all accurate. The only "factual" problem is the uncited and one-sided childhood-amnesia attribution in §9, which is better classed as an overstated claim than a flat error.

## Prose and insight
**Alive:** The opening is strong — "a tape recorder left running in an empty room teaches an infant almost nothing" and "the recursion is the point" do real work. "The 'someone' the caregiver addresses is, at first, a useful fiction … acting on it generates the contingencies that eventually make it true" (§5) is the paper's best single idea, crisply put. "The adult scientist is the infant plus an internalized civilization" (§11) lands. The deprivation section's closing sentence ("Deprivation does not reveal what a human is in the absence of culture … It reveals that the human organism expects a social, linguistic, and cultural niche") is genuinely good.

**Dead:** The "control migrates inward" / "from outside in" refrain is repeated past the point of effect. The three tables (§2 layers, §5 adult functions, §13 mapping) overlap heavily and could be two. The §13 pseudocode is the weakest passage — it manufactures the appearance of formalization while explicitly disclaiming that it is a model, an executable, or a neural claim; it adds connotation, not content, and a tough referee will read it as decoration.

**Genuine contribution:** A clear, honest, well-sourced *consolidation* of co-regulation, joint attention, language, norms, narrative, executive function, and cumulative culture into a single experience-expectant layering, with the deprivation pattern read as a double dissociation. That is worth publishing as a synthesis. What it is not — despite the framing — is a novel architecture that beats the existing interactionist accounts, because those accounts are neither cited nor outperformed.

## Top fixes
1. **Engage the real rivals.** Replace (or supplement) the nativism/environmentalism foils with the actual competitors — neuroconstructivism (Karmiloff-Smith; Mareschal et al.) and developmental systems theory / probabilistic epigenesis (Gottlieb, Oyama) — and state explicitly what the bootstrapping architecture adds over them. Without this the "neither/nor" framing is a strawman and the novelty claim is unearned.
2. **Separate novel predictions from retrodictions in §14.** Mark which predictions are already-confirmed constituents (joint attention→language; reminiscing→narrative) and which are genuinely new risk for the *integrated* model (e.g., mediation claims, cross-layer interactions). State at least one observation that would falsify the architecture.
3. **Fix the childhood-amnesia paragraph (§9).** Drop or hedge "rather than the development of memory storage as such"; acknowledge the neurogenic and consolidation accounts and present the integrated "these act together" view. Add a citation.
4. **Soften the §11 causal-decomposition overclaim.** "Principally … far more than brain size or general intelligence" is not licensed by a 1–5 IQ-point/year schooling effect. Reword to a claim the evidence supports, and acknowledge heritable variation.
5. **Demote the Mauss recapitulation.** Cut it from the abstract and conclusion as a structural claim; keep it as a one-line suggestive parallel. As stated it is an imposed analogy carrying recapitulationist baggage.
6. **Align "tests" with the paper's own §12 caveats.** In the abstract and elsewhere, change "tests the integrated account against the boundary cases" to "is corroborated by," since the cases cannot discriminate the architecture from rival interactionist accounts.
7. **Trim redundancy.** Collapse the three overlapping tables toward two, thin the "control migrates inward" refrain, and either cut the §13 pseudocode or replace it with a genuine schematic that does explanatory work.
8. **Flag the thin upper stack.** Acknowledge in §11/§14 that the reflective and institutional layers — the ones the title promises — rest on far less evidence than the infancy layers, and frame them as the architecture's least-tested commitments.
