## §3 — Newborn as embodied proto-agent

- [T1] Johnson, Dziurawiec, Ellis & Morton (1991), *Cognition* 40(1-2):1-19. Newborns preferentially track face-like configurations (and the bias declines). — supports the social-orienting-bias claim. (Title verified: includes "and its subsequent decline.")
- [T1] Cooper & Aslin (1990), *Child Development* 61(5):1584-1595. Newborns and 1-month-olds prefer infant-directed speech. — social-orienting bias.
- [T1] Vouloumanos & Werker (2007), *Developmental Science* 10(2):159-164. Neonates prefer speech to closely matched non-speech. — social-orienting bias toward language.
- [T1] Mehler et al. (1988), *Cognition* 29(2):143-178. Young infants discriminate the native language by rhythm. — early native-language sensitivity.
- [T1] Simion, Regolin & Bulf (2008), *PNAS* 105(2):809-813. Two-day-old newborns prefer biological-motion displays. — social-orienting bias.
- [T1] Saffran, Aslin & Newport (1996), *Science* 274(5294):1926-1928. 8-month-olds segment a continuous artificial language by transitional probability after ~2 min exposure. — statistical-learning substrate. (Age and 2-minute exposure verified.)
- [T2] Friston (2010), *Nat Rev Neurosci* 11(2):127-138; Clark (2013), *BBS* 36(3):181-204. Free-energy / predictive-processing framing of agents as prediction-error minimizers. — the predictive-processing frame for §3.
- [T2] Köster, Kayhan, Langeloh & Hoehl (2020), *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 15(3):562-571. Unifies infant statistical, motor, and physical/social learning as prediction-error minimization. — the unifying frame.
- [T1] Oostenbroek et al. (2016), *Current Biology* 26(10):1334-1338. Large longitudinal study finds no reliable neonatal imitation across the first nine weeks. — the "neonatal imitation is contested" hedge.
- [T1] Meltzoff et al. (2018), *Developmental Science* 21(4):e12609. Re-analysis disputes the Oostenbroek null. — the null is itself contested.
- [T2] Davis et al. (2021), *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 16(6):1373-1394. Meta-analysis of 336 effect sizes: positive pooled estimate with substantial heterogeneity. — "meta-analytic estimates are heterogeneous."
- [T2] Wellman, Cross & Watson (2001), *Child Development* 72(3):655-684. Meta-analysis: explicit false belief consolidates ~age 4. — explicit ToM timing.
- [T1] Onishi & Baillargeon (2005), *Science* 308(5719):255-258. 15-month-olds show implicit false-belief sensitivity in looking time. — earlier implicit sensitivity. (Title is a question; initials K. H. verified.)
- [T1] Lewis & Brooks-Gunn (1979), *Social Cognition and the Acquisition of Self*; Rochat (2003), *Consciousness and Cognition* 12(4):717-731. Visual self-recognition / graded self-awareness in the second year. — emergence of the objective self.

## §4 — Co-regulation before self-regulation

- [T2] Atzil, Gao, Fradkin & Barrett (2018), *Nature Human Behaviour* 2(9):624-636. The social brain grows through caregiver allostatic regulation of the infant's interoceptive state. — physiological-before-psychological co-regulation.
- [T2] Taipale (2016), *Frontiers in Psychology* 7:889. Mature affect self-regulation develops by internalizing caregiver regulation. — dyadic→individual regulation.
- [T1] Bowlby (1969); Ainsworth et al. (1978). Secure base / safe haven; internal working models. — attachment as a learned model of the regulator.
- [T1] **[CORRECTION]** De Wolff & van IJzendoorn (1997), *Child Development* 68(4):571-591. Sensitivity-security association is r = .22 across **30 studies, N = 1,666**; the 66-study / N=4,176 figure is the full meta across **all** parental antecedents. Draft had wrongly attached 66/4,176 to sensitivity alone. — corrected in §4.
- [T2] Cittern, Nolte, Friston & Edalat (2018), *PLOS ONE* 13(4):e0193955. Secure/avoidant/ambivalent patterns as policies minimizing free energy over interoceptive stress states. — active-inference reading of attachment (mechanism specified as interoceptive).

## §5 — Social address; affect mirroring; external runtime

- [T1] Gergely & Watson (1996), *Int. J. Psycho-Analysis* 77(6):1181-1212. Social-biofeedback theory: marked, contingent affect-mirroring builds categorized emotion representations. — affect mirroring; markedness verified.
- [T2] Fonagy, Gergely, Jurist & Target (2002), *Affect Regulation, Mentalization, and the Development of the Self*. Marked mirroring + caregiver mentalizing scaffold affect regulation and mental-state representation. — the mentalization account.
- [T2] Mauss (1985 [1938]), in Carrithers, Collins & Lukes (eds.), pp. 1-25. "The person" as a category assembled across the history of societies. — relational/anthropological base of personhood; the historical-stratification mirror.

## §6 — Joint attention and shared intentionality

- [T2] Trevarthen & Aitken (2001), *J. Child Psychol. Psychiatry* 42(1):3-48. Primary/secondary intersubjectivity precedes joint attention. — proto-conversation before joint attention.
- [T1] Carpenter, Nagell & Tomasello (1998), *Monographs SRCD* 63(4, Serial No. 255). Emergence of joint-attention skills 9-15 months, tied to early language. — joint attention as platform.
- [T1] Liszkowski et al. (2004), *Developmental Science* 7(3):297-307. 12-month-olds point declaratively to share attention/interest. — declarative pointing as early shared intentionality.
- [T1] Kuhl, Tsao & Liu (2003), *PNAS* 100(15):9096-9101. ~9-month-olds learn Mandarin phonetics from live interaction but not audio/AV recordings. — social interaction needed for early language.
- [T1] **[CORRECTION]** Csibra & Gergely (2009), *Trends Cogn. Sci.* 13(4):148-153. Ostensive cues are eye contact, infant-directed speech, contingent responsivity; **pointing is referential, not ostensive**. Draft had listed pointing as an ostensive cue. — corrected in §6.
- [T2] Tomasello et al. (2005), *BBS* 28(5):675-735; Tomasello (2019); Tomasello, Kruger & Ratner (1993), *BBS* 16(3):495-552. Shared intentionality; imitative/instructed/collaborative cultural learning. — the "we" layer and cultural learning forms.

## §7 — Language as symbolic control architecture

- [T2] Nelson (1996), *Language in Cognitive Development*. Language as a semiotic medium restructuring memory, concepts, narrative, ToM. — language as architecture, not ornament.
- [T1] Mayberry & Lock (2003), *Brain and Language* 87(3):369-384. Early accessible language (signed or spoken) enables later high L2 competence; its absence is poorly compensated. Key variable is accessibility, not modality. — language as developmental infrastructure / sensitive period.

## §8 — Norm internalization and moral agency

- [T1] Rakoczy, Warneken & Tomasello (2008), *Developmental Psychology* 44(3):875-881. 2- and 3-year-olds spontaneously enforce game norms on a third party (puppet). — third-party norm enforcement = internalized general standard. (Ages verified.)
- [T1] Lyons, Young & Keil (2007), *PNAS* 104(50):19751-19756; Horner & Whiten (2005), *Animal Cognition* 8(3):164-181. Children overimitate causally unnecessary steps; high-fidelity copying distinguishes children from apes. — norm-sensitive cultural copying.
- [T2] Thompson (2006), *Handbook of Child Psychology* (6th ed., Vol. 3), pp. 24-98. Conscience within the matrix of social understanding, relationships, self. — moral personhood layer.
- [T1] Lewis, Sullivan, Stanger & Weiss (1989), *Child Development* 60(1):146-156. Self-conscious emotions onset with mirror self-recognition; presuppose a represented self + standard. — guilt/shame/pride require the objective self.

## §9 — Autobiographical memory and narrative selfhood

- [T2] Nelson & Fivush (2004), *Psychological Review* 111(2):486-511. Autobiographical memory emerges from convergence of language, adult memory talk, temporal understanding, self/other understanding. — co-authored narrative self.

## §12 — Deprivation as a boundary condition

- [T1] Nelson et al. (2007), *Science* 318(5858):1937-1940. BEIP RCT: institutional care depressed cognition; earlier foster placement (~<24 mo) improved cognitive outcomes. — institutional-deprivation evidence (cognition + timing only; see split below).
- [T1] **[CORRECTION/split]** Bos et al. (2009), *Front. Behav. Neurosci.* 3:16 (memory/EF); Vanderwert et al. (2010), *PLoS ONE* 5(7):e11415 (EEG, normalizes with earlier placement). Draft had lumped memory/EF/EEG under Nelson 2007; these belong to the follow-ups. — corrected citations in §12.
- [T1] Sonuga-Barke et al. (2017), *The Lancet* 389(10078):1539-1548. ERA cohort: deprivation beyond ~6 months leaves effects into young adulthood. — independent second deprivation cohort.
- [T2] Greenough, Black & Wallace (1987), *Child Development* 58(3):539-559. Experience-expectant vs experience-dependent development. — the experience-expectant framing.
- [T1] Curtiss (1977), *Genie*. Confined child; vocabulary without normal grammar; confounded by abuse/possible disability. — best-documented isolation case, with caveats.
- [T2] Candland (1993), *Feral Children and Clever Animals*. Feral-child cases too poorly documented to settle claims about human nature. — caution; corroborative only.
- [T1] Hall (2017), *Maternal and Child Health Journal* 21(5):961-965. Limited early sign access in deaf children risks language deprivation (dysfluent language, reduced knowledge, disrupted reasoning). — clinical language-deprivation evidence.
