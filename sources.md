# Sources

The frozen bibliography for *From Proto-Agent to Person*, verified on 2026-06-07
(see audit.md). Mirrors the paper's `## References` verbatim. Every in-text
`et al.` resolves to a co-authored entry here (checked by `papers refs`).
Provenance note after each entry: where verified, what it supports.

## Bibliography

Ainsworth, M. D. S., Blehar, M. C., Waters, E., & Wall, S. (1978). *Patterns of Attachment: A Psychological Study of the Strange Situation*. Lawrence Erlbaum. — standard reference; supports the secure-base / attachment-pattern claim (§4).

Atzil, S., Gao, W., Fradkin, I., & Barrett, L. F. (2018). Growing a social brain. *Nature Human Behaviour*, 2(9), 624-636. — verified via DOI 10.1038/s41562-018-0384-6; supports allostatic co-regulation of interoceptive state (§4).

Bos, K. J., Fox, N., Zeanah, C. H., & Nelson, C. A. (2009). Effects of early psychosocial deprivation on the development of memory and executive function. *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience*, 3, 16. — verified via DOI 10.3389/neuro.08.016.2009; supports the BEIP memory/EF deficits (§12, split from Nelson 2007).

Bowlby, J. (1969). *Attachment and Loss, Vol. 1: Attachment*. Basic Books. — foundational; supports attachment as a learned model of the regulator (§4).

Boyd, R., & Richerson, P. J. (1985). *Culture and the Evolutionary Process*. University of Chicago Press. — foundational dual-inheritance theory; supports the cultural-evolution framing (§11).

Candland, D. K. (1993). *Feral Children and Clever Animals: Reflections on Human Nature*. Oxford University Press. — supports the feral-child caution (§12).

Carpenter, M., Nagell, K., & Tomasello, M. (1998). Social cognition, joint attention, and communicative competence from 9 to 15 months of age. *Monographs of the Society for Research in Child Development*, 63(4, Serial No. 255). — verified (JSTOR 10.2307/1166214; authorship is only Carpenter, Nagell & Tomasello); supports joint attention as a platform (§6).

Cittern, D., Nolte, T., Friston, K., & Edalat, A. (2018). Intrinsic and extrinsic motivators of attachment under active inference. *PLOS ONE*, 13(4), e0193955. — verified via DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0193955; supports the active-inference reading of attachment as interoceptive free-energy minimization (§4).

Clark, A. (2013). Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(3), 181-204. — verified via DOI 10.1017/S0140525X12000477; supports the predictive-processing frame (§3).

Cooper, R. P., & Aslin, R. N. (1990). Preference for infant-directed speech in the first month after birth. *Child Development*, 61(5), 1584-1595. — verified (JSTOR 10.2307/1130766); supports the infant-directed-speech preference (§3).

Csibra, G., & Gergely, G. (2009). Natural pedagogy. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 13(4), 148-153. — verified via DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2009.01.005; supports ostensive cues (gaze, IDS, contingency; pointing is referential not ostensive) (§6).

Curtiss, S. (1977). *Genie: A Psycholinguistic Study of a Modern-Day "Wild Child."* Academic Press. — supports the best-documented isolation case with caveats (§12).

Davis, J., Redshaw, J., Suddendorf, T., Nielsen, M., Kennedy-Costantini, S., Oostenbroek, J., & Slaughter, V. (2021). Does neonatal imitation exist? Insights from a meta-analysis of 336 effect sizes. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 16(6), 1373-1394. — verified via DOI 10.1177/1745691620959834; supports the heterogeneity of neonatal-imitation estimates (§3).

De Wolff, M. S., & van IJzendoorn, M. H. (1997). Sensitivity and attachment: A meta-analysis on parental antecedents of infant attachment. *Child Development*, 68(4), 571-591. — verified via DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8624.1997.tb04218.x; sensitivity-security r = .22 (30 studies, N=1,666); 66-study pool covers all antecedents (§4).

Fonagy, P., Gergely, G., Jurist, E. L., & Target, M. (2002). *Affect Regulation, Mentalization, and the Development of the Self*. Other Press. — supports the mentalization account of marked mirroring (§5).

Friston, K. (2010). The free-energy principle: A unified brain theory? *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 127-138. — verified via DOI 10.1038/nrn2787; supports the free-energy framing of the proto-agent (§3).

Gallagher, S. (2000). Philosophical conceptions of the self: Implications for cognitive science. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 4(1), 14-21. — verified via DOI 10.1016/S1364-6613(99)01417-5; supports the minimal-vs-narrative self distinction (§2).

Gergely, G., & Watson, J. S. (1996). The social biofeedback theory of parental affect-mirroring: The development of emotional self-awareness and self-control in infancy. *International Journal of Psycho-Analysis*, 77(6), 1181-1212. — verified (PMID 9119582); supports marked affect-mirroring (§5).

Greenough, W. T., Black, J. E., & Wallace, C. S. (1987). Experience and brain development. *Child Development*, 58(3), 539-559. — canonical source for experience-expectant vs experience-dependent development; supports the experience-expectant framing (§12).

Hall, W. C. (2017). What you don't know can hurt you: The risk of language deprivation by impairing sign language development in deaf children. *Maternal and Child Health Journal*, 21(5), 961-965. — verified via DOI 10.1007/s10995-017-2287-y; supports clinical language-deprivation evidence (§12).

Henrich, J. (2016). *The Secret of Our Success: How Culture Is Driving Human Evolution, Domesticating Our Species, and Making Us Smarter*. Princeton University Press. — supports cumulative culture / dual inheritance (§11).

Horner, V., & Whiten, A. (2005). Causal knowledge and imitation/emulation switching in chimpanzees (*Pan troglodytes*) and children (*Homo sapiens*). *Animal Cognition*, 8(3), 164-181. — verified via DOI 10.1007/s10071-004-0239-6; supports the human-ape copying-fidelity contrast (§8).

Johnson, M. H., Dziurawiec, S., Ellis, H., & Morton, J. (1991). Newborns' preferential tracking of face-like stimuli and its subsequent decline. *Cognition*, 40(1-2), 1-19. — verified (PMID 1786670; full title incl. "and its subsequent decline"); supports the newborn face bias (§3).

Köster, M., Kayhan, E., Langeloh, M., & Hoehl, S. (2020). Making sense of the world: Infant learning from a predictive processing perspective. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 15(3), 562-571. — verified via DOI 10.1177/1745691619895071; supports the unifying predictive-processing frame (§3).

Kuhl, P. K., Tsao, F.-M., & Liu, H.-M. (2003). Foreign-language experience in infancy: Effects of short-term exposure and social interaction on phonetic learning. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 100(15), 9096-9101. — verified via DOI 10.1073/pnas.1532872100; supports the social-interaction requirement for early language (§6).

Lewis, M., & Brooks-Gunn, J. (1979). *Social Cognition and the Acquisition of Self*. Plenum Press. — landmark mirror/rouge test; supports emergence of the objective self in the second year (§3).

Lewis, M., Sullivan, M. W., Stanger, C., & Weiss, M. (1989). Self development and self-conscious emotions. *Child Development*, 60(1), 146-156. — verified (PMID 2702864; JSTOR 10.2307/1131080); supports self-conscious emotions presupposing a represented self (§8).

Liszkowski, U., Carpenter, M., Henning, A., Striano, T., & Tomasello, M. (2004). Twelve-month-olds point to share attention and interest. *Developmental Science*, 7(3), 297-307. — verified (PMID 15595371); supports declarative pointing as early shared intentionality (§6).

Lyons, D. E., Young, A. G., & Keil, F. C. (2007). The hidden structure of overimitation. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 104(50), 19751-19756. — verified via DOI 10.1073/pnas.0704452104; supports overimitation as norm-sensitive copying (§8).

Mauss, M. (1985). A category of the human mind: The notion of person; the notion of self (W. D. Halls, Trans.). In M. Carrithers, S. Collins, & S. Lukes (Eds.), *The Category of the Person: Anthropology, Philosophy, History* (pp. 1-25). Cambridge University Press. (Original work published 1938) — supports the person as a historically assembled category (§5, §11).

Mayberry, R. I., & Lock, E. (2003). Age constraints on first versus second language acquisition: Evidence for linguistic plasticity and epigenesis. *Brain and Language*, 87(3), 369-384. — verified (PMID 14642540); supports early accessible language as infrastructure (§7, §12).

Mehler, J., Jusczyk, P., Lambertz, G., Halsted, N., Bertoncini, J., & Amiel-Tison, C. (1988). A precursor of language acquisition in young infants. *Cognition*, 29(2), 143-178. — verified (PMID 3168420); supports native-language rhythm discrimination (§3).

Meltzoff, A. N., Murray, L., Simpson, E., Heimann, M., Nagy, E., Nadel, J., Pedersen, E. J., Brooks, R., Messinger, D. S., De Pascalis, L., Subiaul, F., Paukner, A., & Ferrari, P. F. (2018). (2016): Evidence for neonatal imitation of tongue protrusion. *Developmental Science*, 21(4), e12609. — verified via DOI 10.1111/desc.12609; supports "the null is contested" (§3).

Nelson, C. A., Zeanah, C. H., Fox, N. A., Marshall, P. J., Smyke, A. T., & Guthrie, D. (2007). Nelson, K. (1996). *Language in Cognitive Development: The Emergence of the Mediated Mind*. Cambridge University Press. — supports language as a mediating semiotic medium (§7).

Nelson, K., & Fivush, R. (2004). The emergence of autobiographical memory: A social cultural developmental theory. *Psychological Review*, 111(2), 486-511. — verified (PMID 15065919); supports the co-authored narrative self (§9).

Rakoczy, H., Warneken, F., & Tomasello, M. (2008). The sources of normativity: Young children's awareness of the normative structure of games. *Developmental Psychology*, 44(3), 875-881. — verified (PMID 18473651; ages 2-3); supports third-party norm enforcement (§8).

Rochat, P. (2003). Five levels of self-awareness as they unfold early in life. *Consciousness and Cognition*, 12(4), 717-731. — verified via DOI 10.1016/S1053-8100(03)00081-3; supports graded self-awareness (§3).
